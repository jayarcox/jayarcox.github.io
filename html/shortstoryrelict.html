Relict

by
J.R. Cox

1

Sarah opened her eyes. The soft warmth of the midday sun reminded her of how she used to nap on the porch after lunch in autumn before school started. She blinked the fatigue from her eyes. She overslept. She instinctually reached for the pocket of her backpack that she used as a pillow and found Sam, her travelling companion, right where she left him. Sam was a well-worn stuffed bear, just a little too big to fit inside a coffee mug, but the perfect size to peak his head out of the water bottle pocket on the side of Sarah’s pack.

“You’re looking threadbare,” she said aloud to him, and pretended he rolled his eyes at the pun.

Reassured that he was with her, she rose, tightened the strings on her fashionable but quickly decomposing combat boots and set out across the valley floor. She ran even though the rock and sage covered terrain was rough on her soles, but she had no time to lose.

“Hold on tight, Sam,” she said as she slid over some loose rocks. The less time spent in the open the better. She scrambled up a hill and at the top she saw what she was looking for. “See? What did I tell you?”

It wasn’t more than little burnt spot in the middle of some trampled sage. She thought she had seen the light from the fire the night prior and immediately made her way for it. A fire means a camp. A camp means people, and people mean she might make it. When you think of the word survivor, Sarah wasn’t who you’d picture.

She used to play games on social media sites where she’d pick which of her friends she’d want to join her in the zombie apocalypse. She was never on anyone’s list. It seemed silly at the time. She would never see any of those people again, but the real tragic irony is that Sarah was a people person. Her first thought when she saw the newscast of San Francisco being melted into the Earth by some mysterious bomb was how sad it was that she would never get to meet any of those poor people.

Soon after that swift, mysterious attack, the creatures came. Sarah and her parents fled to the nearest city to find her aunt, but all they saw when they arrived was bubbles of black rock where civilization once stood. It became obvious very quickly that even moderately small cities were targets of the bombs, and anywhere people gathered, the creatures would soon be.

So Sarah, like all the smartest of humanity fled to the wilderness. But Sarah knew she couldn’t make it by herself, and as her stomach grumbled she ran with renewed vigor in the direction of the trampled foliage.

The trail seemed to lead up to the mountains, and as the sun began to dip behind the mountains, she finally caught sight of them. She was so happy she could cry, but she suppressed the urge. Tears aren’t social, she thought.

“Stop,” came the sudden cry from the man on guard. She halted, searching the trees for the source of the voice.

“Who are you?” the man called.

“My name is Sarah, and this is Sam.” She held her pack aloft so the bear peeked out. She grabbed his little paw and waved hello to no one in particular. She couldn’t quite see where the voice was coming from. Then she heard footsteps and murmurs.

Another voice echoed down from the trees higher up the mountain, “Do you have any skills or equipment?” This voice was softer and older, but still rugged. More like a grandpa than the first voice.

“Um, we don’t have any equipment, other than the backpack and a cup, and I don’t really have any skills, but Sam used to do standup,” she smiled in the general direction of the voices. She heard more murmuring and soon the two men stepped out of the forest to greet her.

The two men were very serious. She could tell the one who spoke second was the leader. He had gray hair and walked in front. He was first to extend his hand and speak again, “I’m Rizzo. This is Foley. You don’t look like you’re going to try and hurt anyone, but if you try to steal anything from us, we will find you and get it back, and punish you in a way that fits the crime. Understand?”

Sarah enthusiastically nodded her head. Her unruly red hair bouncing with each nod did not help to disguise her excitement.

“But you’re going to have to work to live with us too. Everyone in our camp carries their own weight and extra as needed. You fall behind, we’ll leave you to die. That’s just the way it’s gotta be,” he said.

“Absolutely,” she responded as if at an interview,” I totally get it. I’m a quick learner, and you won’t regret this one bit.”

Moments later she was collecting firewood. Aside from Rizzo and Foley, there were almost a dozen people on the mountainside. She was quickly introduced to the other women, and was already making friends. It all came streaming back to her as if it were her first day at college again. She never had difficulty getting along with strangers and easily turned them into friends. She was especially enjoying the company of Rizzo’s oldest, and portliest, daughter, Tiffany, and her young daughter, Belle.

Sarah was able to quickly dispel any of Belle’s shyness with a little help from Sam, and before long they were best friends. Belle was so upbeat. Sarah couldn’t have expected the same melancholy she saw in everyone else to translate to a sixyear-old, but that didn’t make it any less refreshing. Belle was immediately telling Sarah everything she needed to know about living in the wilderness, right down to directing which sticks she should pick up for firewood. Sarah giggled at the energetic specificity of the information, although she wasn’t committing a lot of it to memory.

“And the birch sticks look like this and they catch really fast, but they burn out quick too because they’re so light. You’re really pretty, you know that?” Belle blurted out without provocation. Sarah laughed and blushed at the unexpected compliment.

“Thank you,” she said magnanimously.

“Wanna play a game!?”

“Yeah. Of course,” Sarah said as if it were the only possible response.

“Okay. We’re going to play hide and seek. Mommy says I have to play it everyday, so I get good at hiding.”

Sarah looked at Tiffany and smiled, “Your mommy is a very smart lady,” she said. She wasn’t above flattering people.

“K. Count to a hundred. I’ll go hide.” And she was off like a shot. Tiffany picked up a stick near to Sarah. “Thanks for being so nice to her. I know she talks a lot.” Tiffany said.

“No, no. It’s really refreshing. It’s been a long time since I’ve heard anyone talking,” Sarah said, congenially.

“I’m just happy to have someone good with kids. Some of the guys around here don’t have a lot of patience for her.”

“I understand. But she’s really a delightful little girl. And smart.”

“I hope so,” Tiffany paused for a moment, “She’s starting to really like you. I hope you stick around.”

Sarah shifted her sticks to one arm and placed an encouraging hand on Tiffany’s shoulder. “I hope so too.”

After the game was over, the sticks were gathered and night had set about the camp in earnest, Sarah was given the first real meal she had eaten in weeks. Some of the camp members happened upon a warehouse near an air force base where they were able to scavenge a bunch of combat rations. It wasn’t great, but it was so much better than random berries and hopefully edible twigs. Sarah was starting to feel at home for the first time since the world had changed. After supper Belle gave her a real pillow and blanket and she curled up between the roots of a tree.

People stayed up for a bit, but soon all the noise was gone and the flashlights turned out. Sarah smiled at Sam sitting limply in the pocket of her pack that was for the first time in weeks not under her head as she slept.

Then she felt someone kick her boot. A lighter flickered on, and she saw it was Foley.

“Foley? What are you doing?” she asked through squinted eyes. He held a finger up to his lips, and crouched down next to her.

“You know you’re really pretty,” he whispered, and Sarah suddenly new exactly where this was going.

“Thanks, Foley. I’ll see you in the morning.”

“Look. There’s a lot of reasons why we can’t wait until we fall in love. With the world the way it is, you know?”

“Foley. I’ll scream,” she said with a surprising amount of composure. Foley cursed under his breath and leaned back a bit to Sarah’s relief, but then he pulled a knife out of his boot. Suddenly Sarah’s heart started pounding. She didn’t know what to do. Foley flicked off the lighter.

“Foley. Please,” she said now with crying starting to warble in her throat.

“I’m sorry. I hate that it has to be like this too. I just- we’ll make it quick.” She could hear him undoing his pants with his one free hand.

“Foley. Wait.”

She could feel him jerking her belt around trying to loosen it. She tried grabbing his hands but felt the knife pressing more and more firmly against her neck until she stopped.

“Stop. Okay. Wait.”

Finally he struggled to pull her pants down with her trying to clench her thighs together.

“Foley. No. Not in front of Sam,” she said trying to smile, but Foley just kept jerking at her pants.

All she could do as Foley’s strength overpowered her own was turn her head and look at the moonlight reflecting in Sam’s beadlike eyes as the tears started to stream from her own.

Suddenly the struggle stopped. Droplets of blood speckled Sarah’s face. The loud boom that accompanied the feeling seemed to put the world in slow motion. She turned her head away from Sam, and watched Foley’s headless body slump on top of her. She could feel her breath coming in irregular bursts. Lights began popping on throughout the camp, and screams as if from miles away accompanied the deaths of every other person in the camp. She watched from under Foley’s corpse as her new comrades, one after another, fell in the middle of frightened flight.

The destruction caused by the creature’s weapons was beyond reason. She saw sternums blown open from the back, liquefied organs spilling over fractured ribs. Limbs seemingly vaporized with nothing but rivers of blood pouring out where they were once attached.

But the hardest part to watch was Belle ripped from Tiffany’s fighting grasp. The creature’s size and strength was as if a man to a small child, and as soon as Belle was taken Tiffany was hit by two shots at once, sending what was left of her torso spinning into the night sky, all in front of Belle’s furiously crying eyes. Her little arms outstretched, long after her mother’s body was nowhere to be seen, screaming for her as the creatures spirited her away.

And within moments they were gone. Sarah lay, unmoving, frozen in terror for the rest of the night.
