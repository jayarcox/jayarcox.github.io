The Republic of the Great Pacific Garbage Patch

Waded was Jonathon’s first wife. Despite that, it didn’t seem strange at all that there were no rumors. No gossip about what had become of her. Few of us knew her at all and those that did, didn’t know her well. I can’t speak for all of us, but I certainly felt her departure from the Republic was proof that I was there of my own volition. After all, even his first wife left freely. I didn’t bother examining it much deeper than that. If her disappearance was anything other than her own voluntary cessation from our new state, then what would that say about me and my choices? My freedom? My beliefs? I didn’t bother asking those questions then. The most reasonable explanation was also the least frightening. If you think about it logically like that, you start to realize a little bit of how we wound up there. Everyone takes some amount of their reality on faith. You have to. At least some.

The Boy and the Fire

A boy and man were travelling together. As the sun began to get low, the man stopped in a clearing. The man said to the boy, “I will start a fire, and go down to the river to fish.”

The man started the fire, and it was very small and puny. The man then said to the boy, “This fire is week now, but it wants to be strong. Watch that it does not grow and do not feed it. I will come back with fish soon.”

Then the man left, and the boy sat down next to the fire. It was hard for the boy to sit and watch the fire while the man was fishing. The boy wanted to go to the river too, but he did as the man said.

Finally there was a great wind, and the fire waned. The boy became frightened that the fire would go out. If the fire went out, then the boy would have no job to do. Surely the man did not want the boy to let the fire die. So he fed the fire just a little bit to keep it going.

Relict

Sarah opened her eyes. The soft warmth of the midday sun reminded her of how she used to nap on the porch after lunch in autumn before school started. She blinked the fatigue from her eyes. She overslept. She instinctually reached for the pocket of her backpack that she used as a pillow and found Sam, her travelling companion, right where she left him. Sam was a well-worn stuffed bear, just a little too big to fit inside a coffee mug, but the perfect size to peak his head out of the water bottle pocket on the side of Sarah’s pack.

“You’re looking threadbare,” she said aloud to him, and pretended he rolled his eyes at the pun.

Reassured that he was with her, she rose, tightened the strings on her fashionable but quickly decomposing combat boots and set out across the valley floor. She ran even though the rock and sage covered terrain was rough on her soles, but she had no time to lose.

Overtime

You don’t actually hear the sound of your own breathing. I guess you hear it, but it isn’t something you pay attention to. Even when you can’t hear anything else, you just tune it out. Or, I do, rather. I guess I can’t speak for everyone. But even right now, I’m only hearing it because I’m thinking about it. Honestly, I more often get distracted by the sound of the blood pumping through my ears. I like that sound better anyway.

I listen to that sound a lot while I’m working because I work a lot outside. I actually love it out here. I used to talk to people when I went back to the surface. They said that it was frightening. It is frightening, but I think it’s the kind of frightening that God is. You’re scared of it, but you also kind of depend on it. Or, I do, rather. It gives me this sense of… something. You start to get kind of numb when you’re out here a while.

Barnaby the Bulkier

Barnaby the Bulkier was strong and fierce in deed,
When his horse ran over much, he carried the poor steed.
Long of hair and long of beard, and wide of chest and bone,
He traveled o’er the countryside, a-questing for a throne.

Came Barnaby upon a land scourg’d by torrid flames
The cause of which was mystery, or so the story claims.
Thus Barnaby set down his horse and sought to douse the fire,
So people would be grateful and supply his heart’s desire.

He dug a trench from mountain stream to forest as it burn’d,
He toil’d hard but did not tire, or have you not learn’d?
Barnaby had great big arms, his back was broad and stout,
Bulkiness was in his name, it’s what this tale’s about.
