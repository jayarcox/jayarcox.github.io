Bob

by
J.R. Cox

FADE IN...

EXT. YARD - DAY

An apple, freshly fallen from the branch, rests joyfully near the roots.

EXT. GARAGE SALE - DAY

Antiques. Old dressers, lamps, VHS cassettes. Dusty and disused, they are strewn across folding tables on the lawn. Among them, an old hat sits atop a styrofoam head.

TIFFANY (30) scrutinizes a smudged fishbowl while talking on her cellphone.

TIFFANY

You’re sure you don’t need help? I can come in. (moving the phone away from her mouth) What do you think about getting a fish, hon?

ROBBY

That might be nice.

Tiffany absently sets down the fish bowl and turns to another table, revealing ROBBY (33), a balding man, thumbing through a tattered paperback behind her.

TIFFANY

Well if you’re sure... I will. Bye.

She hangs up.

TIFFANY

Ooh.

The old hat catches Tiffany’s eye. She grabs the foam head, and takes it to Robby.

TIFFANY

Here. Try this on.

She moves the hat onto Robby’s head as he recoils in disgust.

ROBBY

Ugh! What are you doing?!

Surprised, Tiffany backs away. Robby removes the hat.

TIFFANY

Seeing how it looks on you?

ROBBY

Who knows who else has worn this. It’s disgusting.

Tiffany rolls her eyes, and grabs the hat back from him, trying to get it back onto his head as he snakes away from it.

TIFFANY

You’re overreacting. It’s a cute hat. And it’s clean, look at it. See? No lice.

She finally corners him and gets it back onto his head.

TIFFANY

Go on, turn around so I can get a proper look.

Defeated, he sulks in a circle.

TIFFANY

Very dashing. And it’ll keep you from burning when you forget sunscreen.

(to the vendor)

How much is this?

The VENDOR (65), her voice as craggy as her face, calls from the shadow of a big straw hat.

VENDOR

It’s free if you buy the hat.

TIFFANY

(Realizing the head is in her hand)

Oh, ha, no. Wait, really?

VENDOR

Sure.

Robby grimaces behind Tiffany.

TIFFANY

How much for the hat?

VENDOR

TIFFANY

I’ll give you 5.

VENDOR

Sold.

Beaming, Tiffany looks for her wallet.

TIFFANY

Can you go get my purse out of the car?

Robby dutifully walks out of sight.

VENDOR

It comes with a warning.

TIFFANY

I’m sorry?

VENDOR

You need to have a strong personality. Give him an inch, and he’ll walk all over you.

TIFFANY

(indicating Robby)

Oh I think I can handle him.

VENDOR

Not him. The head.

TIFFANY

Excuse me?

VENDOR

He’s always looking for a way to get out.

Tiffany looks at the Vendor with generous curiosity. The Vendor looks back, gravely serious.

Then she erupts into a harsh, mucus-y laugh. Robby returns with the wallet, distracting Tiffany.

ROBBY

It’s gonna cost me more than a new hat to get it cleaned.

TIFFANY

Don’t be such a downer. Look we get this fun head too.

ROBBY

God, no. Leave it.

TIFFANY

Why? You’ll need a place for your new hat, and (spooky voiced) it comes with a warning.

ROBBY

It’s creepy enough. Look at it. Terrible. Just leave it.

TIFFANY

You’ll get used to it. Pay the kind woman please.

INT. CAR - DAY

Robby, in the passenger seat, holds the head with the hat on in his lap.

INT. BEDROOM - EVENING

Robby sets the head on a shelf in the closet. Tiffany walks in.

TIFFANY

No, don’t hide it in there.

ROBBY

I’m not hiding it.

Tiffany pulls the head down and sets it on the dresser facing the bed.

TIFFANY

If you put it here you can put it on after you get dressed and you’ll be ready to go out for the day.

Robby moves it back up to the shelf.

ROBBY

I don’t want it there.

TIFFANY

What?

(in a mocking voice)

Are you afraid of it?

ROBBY

(In a similar voice)

No, I’m not afraid of it.

(normal)

I just don’t want it on the dresser.

TIFFANY

Whatever. It belongs to you. If you want hide it in the closet and never look at it again it’s your business.

She exits. Robby looks at it in the closet and sighs.

INT. BEDROOM - NIGHT

The head is on the dresser.

Robby rolls over in bed. He looks at the head. It almost seems to look back at him. Robby hitches the covers up and turns away from it.

EXT. YARD - LATER

The apple shows signs of decay.

INT. BEDROOM - DAY

Robby sits on the bed pulling his socks up. He looks at the head.

Tiffany walks into the bedroom with wet hair, brushing her teeth.

TIFFANY

What are you going to do today?

ROBBY

I don’t know. No plans.

TIFFANY

You should go out.

ROBBY

Why?

TIFFANY

I don’t know. What do you even do in the house all day?

ROBBY

Hang out I guess. I did dishes yesterday.

TIFFANY

All day?

ROBBY

What do you want me to say?

Tiffany exits. Robby hears the sounds of spitting and the faucet.

TIFFANY

I have to go to work. There’s a lot I need to get done today. It would be nice to hear what your day was like when I get home.

She throws on a cardigan and leaves Robby alone in the bedroom. He looks back at the head.

ROBBY

You are kind of scary.

He continues pondering the head for a beat.

ROBBY

I’ve got an idea.

He grabs the head.

INT. HOME OFFICE - DAY

Towers of boxes fill the room labeled “Stationary”, “Bumper Stickers”, “Magnets” but most with the label “Bottles”.

A pen with a hand applied sticker featuring the logo “Apple Bob’s Fine Cider” rests over a marked up newspaper classified. Envelopes from myriad colleges litter the desk, some unopened. Robby enters and brushes them aside with one hand, the head gripped in his other. He hunts through drawers and bins to find glue and a pack of googly eyes. The hat flops off the head onto the floor.

He meticulously applies glue to the googly eyes and, concentrating hard, places them carefully over the head’s blank sockets.

ROBBY

There. Now you’re a bit silly, aren’t you?

One of the pupils in the googly eye droops.

ROBBY

You’re still pretty scary though.

Sighing, Robby turns to exit the room and steps on the hat.

ROBBY

Great.

He bends down to pick it up and brushes some of the dust off of it.

INT. DRY CLEANER - DAY

Robby holds the head in his hand as he waits at the counter. The Dry Cleaner emerges from the back with the freshly laundered hat in a bag.

DRY CLEANER

Here you go. That’ll be $4.50.

Robby takes the hat back from him, then has to set it on the counter to fish out his wallet.

DRY CLEANER

Who’s your friend there?

Robby pauses, bewildered, and then double takes at the head in his hand, as if noticing it for the first time.

ROBBY

I didn’t realize it was still... I guess maybe I’m preoccupied.

DRY CLEANER

Sure. It’s just you had it when you dropped this off a few hours ago too.

ROBBY

You're kidding.

He hands over a few bucks and the Dry Cleaner opens the register.

DRY CLEANER

(still chuckling over it)

When you came back in with it I figured you must be holding it for a reason.

He hands Robby his receipt. Robby stares contemplatively at the head.

ROBBY

No. I don’t think so.

He looks back at the now dubious Dry Cleaner.

ROBBY

Thank you.

He leaves.

INT. KITCHEN - EVENING

Robby rends open a bag of cilantro and savages it with a knife. A pot steams on the range.

He reacts to sounds from the front door opening and closing as Tiffany enters the home in a bustle.

ROBBY

(shouting)

Hey babe, I’m in here.

TIFFANY (O.S.)

Hey, hon. I brought home dinner. Where- are you in the kitchen?

Tiffany walks into the kitchen still with her coat on sets a greasy paper bag on the counter. She smiles brightly at Robby when she sees him and puts a hand around his waist as she looks into the pot. She takes no notice of the three bowls on the counter.

TIFFANY

Oh my god, it is so sweet of you to make dinner. I wouldn’t have picked anything up if I’d known. It smells delicious.

She disengages and pulls up her phone as she steps back toward the other room.

ROBBY

I had a really bizarre day. I don’t know. It’s like I don’t quite feel like myself.

He looks away from the pot and sees she is responding to a text. He waits for her to finish.

TIFFANY

How was your day hon?

ROBBY

I went to the dry cleaner—

DING. A notification from her phone.

ROBBY

—and you ever have one of those moments where you’re looking everywhere for something you have in your hand—

She chuckles. He inhales, about to elaborate, when he looks and sees she was laughing at the text which she now responds to. He waits. This one takes a few edits before she sends. She nearly puts it in her bag.

Another DING.

Another response. Robby starts serving up chili, and fills two of the bowls.

Finally—

TIFFANY

I’m so sorry. Busy, busy day. So what did you make?

ROBBY

Chili.

TIFFANY

I wish I could stay and have dinner with you. Remember the board was meeting tonight and I can’t get out of it.

She grabs a burger out of the bag.

ROBBY

Oh no. What was this one about?

TIFFANY

I actually need to leave like five minutes ago. I just wanted to drop off food for you, but you’re already set. I’ll text you if it looks like I’ll be late.

She takes a moment to regard Robby for the first time. She tenderly puts her hand on his cheek and looks into his eyes.

TIFFANY

Hey. I really do appreciate you making this for us. I love you.

ROBBY

I love you too.

She kisses him and heads out of the room.

TIFFANY (O.S.)

And I still want to hear about your day!

ROBBY

Drive sa—

The door slams.

Robby, hands full of chili bowls adjourns to the dining room.

INT. DINING ROOM - CONTINUOUS

The head sits at a place at the table, the hat, still in the plastic bag from the cleaner, on its head. Robby takes a seat across from it, setting a bowl in front of himself. He realizes suddenly he brought the other bowl as well and slides it over to the head with a disappointed sigh.

ROBBY

Bon appetit.

INT. BEDROOM - NIGHT

Robby lies in the bed, awake. He tosses and turns a few times before glancing up at the dresser.

The head’s new eyes glint slightly from the street lamp outside, making the sense that it is looking back at him even stronger. Its glossy styrofoam skin reflects brightly in the dim light.

Robby sits up and sighs.

INT. GARAGE - NIGHT

Robby, in his pajamas, draws a brush of pink paint over the surface of the head, carefully avoiding the eyes and hat. At his feet paint cans of red, yellow and white are open.

ROBBY

Looks like we’re going to have to get used to each other. Maybe we should give you a name.

He holds it away from him to inspect the overall affect.

ROBBY

Yeah. You’re gonna need some eyebrows, Bob.

EXT. DRIVEWAY - NIGHT

Tiffany’s car pulls in. She steps out and walks into the house.

INT. FRONT HALL - NIGHT

She steps in and finds the lights are still on. She sets down her bag and starts taking off her jacket when she hears voices and noises from the Home Office.

ROBBY (O.S.)

Stacked up like that they just constantly reminded me, Bob, day after day.

Shattering glass. She steps toward the sound to investigate.

INT. HOME OFFICE - NIGHT

Tiffany enters the room to find Robby holding one of the merch boxes, paint all over his shirt, hands and face. In the corner she sees a rough pile of the other boxes.

ROBBY

It’s like living in the ruins of the person you used to think you were before the world let you know what a failure you are.

TIFFANY

Hey, hon. What are you doing up?

Robby jumps and looks up at her with wide eyes, as if caught in the act.

ROBBY

Nothing. Just got into a project while you were out.

TIFFANY

Oh. That’s... great. Are you coming to bed?

ROBBY

Yeah. Yeah. Just give me a minute to clean this up and I’ll be right there.

Still suspicious, Tiffany leaves. Robby sets the box down quietly. He pulls a detail brush out of a drawer and picks the head up out of a chair that hid it from Tiffany’s view. He exits as well.

EXT. YARD - LATER

The apple rots as illumination pours over it from a rising sun.

INT. BEDROOM - MORNING

Tiffany rolls over in the bed reaching for a person, and opens her eyes in surprise when she finds no one.

TIFFANY

Hon?

She blinks sleep out of her eyes.

TIFFANY

(Calling out)

Honey?

INT. GARAGE - MORNING

Robby meticulously adds hair detail to the head, looking back and forth from a mirror as he dips a fine haired brush onto a makeshift pallette.

TIFFANY (O.S.)

Honey? Are you in there?

The door opens, flooding sunlight over Robby’s tired face. Tiffany, in her pajamas, arms folded tightly, squints through the doorway.

ROBBY

Morning.

TIFFANY

What the hell are you doing?

ROBBY

We’re really starting to look alike, aren’t we?

TIFFANY

Do you know what time it is? I had no idea where you were.

ROBBY

Just hanging out with Bob.

Tiffany looks confused, shakes her head and storms off. Robby watches her leave with naive concern, then turns back to the head. It is a very rudimentary portrait.

He looks back at the door for a moment, then continues painting.

INT. KITCHEN - MORNING

Robby enters the back door with Bob under his arm to find Tiffany making breakfast.

ROBBY

Thought we’d come in for a bite.

Tiffany continues aggressively scrambling the eggs.

ROBBY

Maybe we’ll just grab a glass of orange juice.

He moves to a cabinet and pulls out two glasses. Tiffany doesn’t look up from her eggs. He pulls the orange juice from the fridge and pours. She sighs heavily, but doesn’t look at him.

TIFFANY

You want some eggs.

ROBBY

Sure.

She plates some up.

TIFFANY

I woke up and you were just gone. I called for you, looked all over the house.

ROBBY

Sorry. You got in late. I didn’t want to wake you.

She turns, noticing the head in his hands.

TIFFANY

What are you doing with that thing?

ROBBY

What? Drinking orange juice.

She notices the two glasses.

TIFFANY

I mean, why are you painting it?

He smiles and holds it up next to his head to invite the resemblance.

ROBBY

It’s not a good look for Bob?

TIFFANY

Did you name that thing... Bob?

ROBBY

Yeah. I figured he should have a name.

TIFFANY

Your name is Bob.

Bob blinks thoughtfully, as if this is the first time he considered that.

BOB

Hm. Yeah, I guess that is kind of funny.

Her phone rings from another room. Tiffany draws a calming breath.

TIFFANY

I am sorry. I am sorry I bought you that hat. I am sorry that I couldn’t have dinner with you, and I am so sorry that I asked you to bring that thing home. Okay?

The phone keeps ringing.

BOB

You don’t need to apologize. I think we made the best of it.

—and ringing.

TIFFANY

This isn’t funny. I want you to get rid of that thing.

Finally, a jingle indicating a message.

BOB

Aww. No. We’ve really grown attached.

TIFFANY

Stop it. You’ve been so listless, more than listless, ever since your company- I’m starting to think you need real help.

BOB

Lighten up. We’re just having fun.

The phone dings with a text.

TIFFANY

I’m not having fun. I’m really worried about you.

BOB

There’s nothing to worry about.

It dings again. Tiffany looks into Bob’s eyes trying find meaning there where words have failed. When the phone dings again she blinks and walks into the other room.

Bob pours orange juice into both glasses.

TIFFANY (O.S.)

They need me to come in.

BOB

No problem. We’ve got lots to occupy ourselves with today.

Tiffany returns to the entrance of the kitchen putting on her jacket. She pauses halfway through the action, a pained expression on her face. Bob maintains the same detached, content expression.

She puts on her coat and steps up to give him a kiss goodbye.

TIFFANY

We’ll talk more when I get home, but, please, get rid of that thing today. Okay?

She walks out of the kitchen. He watches her until he hears the door shutting behind her.

Bob looks at the head. He pours orange juice over it’s mouth. It spills everywhere.

EXT. YARD - LATER

The Apple festers with mold and insects.

INT. BATHROOM - EVENING

Bob methodically trims hairs from a wig sitting atop the head. He checks the mirror.

BOB

Annnnd—

He makes one more snip.

BOB

Perfect. I can’t tell which one’s the mirror.

He holds up the head to the mirror. There is no resemblance.

BOB

It’s like we’re twins. No one will be able to tell us apart. We could even... switch places.

INT. BEDROOM - EVENING

Bob sets the head back on the dresser and puts the hat back over the newly trimmed wig. He stares at it, and again it seems to stare back.

Bob’s expression subtly changes as he looks at the object from a childlike interest to a more maniacal smirk, the hat somehow on his head now.

EXT. DRIVEWAY - NIGHT

Tiffany’s car pulls into the driveway. She parks and steps out.

INT. FRONT HALL - NIGHT

Tiffany opens the door to a dark house.

TIFFANY

Sorry I’m so late. Bob?

She steps in and looks around.

TIFFANY

Bob? Are you here? I saw your car in the driveway.

She flicks a light switch on. No evidence of Bob aside from a trail of styrofoam crumbs leading to the Kitchen. She follows them.

TIFFANY

Bob?

INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT

She flicks on the light in the kitchen. More styrofoam carnage leads to the back door. She continues to follow the trail. A knife is notably missing from the block.

INT. GARAGE - NIGHT

The door to the garage creeps open. Tiffany enters hesitantly. She flicks on her phone light.

The discarded paint cans and brushes still litter the floor along with pieces of the head. Stacks of leaning lawn equipment and winter tires create long shadows, obscuring large portions of the room from her sight.

TIFFANY

(quietly)

Bob?

She sweeps the light over the garage until she sees the face. It has been mutilated and covered in red paint.

She approaches it and picks it up, utterly disturbed.

The door creaks shut. She jumps, dropping the face. Turning the light in that direction, she finds no one.

TIFFANY

Bob?!

He peaks his head out of the darkness over her shoulder, the hat still on his head.

BOB

We decided you were right.

She screams and falls forward.

TIFFANY

What the hell is happening?!

BOB

We got rid of him.

She scrambles to her feet.

TIFFANY

What are you doing Bob?

BOB

No no.

He bends down and pokes the knife into the dropped face. And slowly lifts it up to look at it.

BOB

I’m not Bob. I’m... Bob.

The face drops from the knife leaving the point menacingly exposed.

Tiffany’s phone dings.

She rolls her eyes and starts typing back. Bob seems surprised but not displeased.

TIFFANY

(exacerbated)

Thank you for listening to me, but please clean up the pieces of that thing. It’s all over the house. I have to make a phone call.

She lifts the phone to her ear and leaves Bob to his devices. He looks down at the mangled face, smiles deviously and steps on it.

EXT. YARD - LATER

The rotted apple dries in bright sunlight. A rake scrapes it up into a pile with other lawn debris. Bob, still in the hat, takes a break and rests on the rake, his creepy smile persisting as he surveys the neighborhood. The remnants of his business await a garbage truck next to the dumpster at the edge of the yard.

He takes a deep, life-affirming breath, and proceeds with the yard work.

FADE OUT.
