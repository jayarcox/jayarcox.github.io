K-6

by
J.R. Cox

EXT. PLAYGROUND - DAY

A swing squeaks in the breeze with haunting rhythm.

EXT. HOUSE - DAY

A minivan pulls into a driveway where a U-haul already rests. A family exits the vehicle. An earnest young boy, TYLER (8-9) grabs a cardboard box out of the back of the van and sighs deeply. MOM notices.

MOM

What’s the matter Tyler?

Tyler attempts to escape into their new house.

MOM

You miss your friends, huh?

Tyler stops and his mother crouches down to his level.

MOM

I know it’s hard moving to a new place, but you’ll see. I bet you're new school is going to be a lot better than you think. Hey! How about we finish unloading the van, and then we can have some ice cream. Does that sound like fun?

TYLER

I guess.

MOM

Alright then. Come on bud.

As they walk into the house Mom tickles him a bit, successfully cheering him up a little.

INT. CAR - EARLY MORNING

DAD and Tyler sit in the van outside of dilapidated elementary school. The sign on the side of the school lacks some letters and bricks lie crumbled from the wall. An intense blizzard rages outside the vehicle.

DAD

That doesn’t look too nice, does it? Well, see you later kiddo.

Dad hurries Tyler out of the vehicle and speeds away.

EXT/INT. SCHOOL - EARLY MORNING

Tyler reluctantly shuffles through the foreboding front doors. He slowly tries to take off his beanie, but someone stops him.

OFFICE LADY

You have to go outside.

TYLER

What?

She leans over the front desk to get a better look at Tyler. Her old fashioned glasses appear to be from the late 1960s. She likely hasn’t updated her prescription since that time.

OFFICE LADY

Your new huh? Well kids aren’t allowed to stay in the building during morning recess.

TYLER

But the weather—

OFFICE LADY

Look kid. This isn’t your old school. Here the kids have to be outside during morning recess. Get it?

TYLER

Yes ma’am.

Tyler, defeated, heads toward the doors that face the playground. He notices a window next to the office that has black construction paper taped over it with block letters spelling out “ZERO TOLERANCE”. The door resists him with a pile of snow and intense winds, but he manages to open it.

EXT. PLAYGROUND - EARLY MORNING

On the playground darkness reigns, as the few light posts are dimmed with the torrent of snow. He travels slowly through the wind and ice toward a light. As he nears it his eyes adjust to expose terrifying scenes. In one direction, a child opens one side of his jacket revealing an “intent to sell” amount of pixie sticks to an onlooker who searches his pockets for cash. In another direction a younger child’s arms are held behind his back as an older child pummels his exposed stomach, chanting a string of curse words all the while. He looks away only to see a small child blowing away in the storm. Walking back toward the school he looks back to the lamp post. He sees a kid collapse from the cold, and nearly immediately after a small “scavenger” carrying an excess of snow gear rushes to loot the body. Finally the whistle blows and suddenly a sea of students surround him, as the population emerges from the darkness. The current washes him in the direction of another door. Finally it sweeps him inside.

INT. SCHOOL HALLWAY - EARLY MORNING

The interior of the school has welcome warmth, but the grim expressions of the myriad people seem to cool the place considerably. A large militaristic man marches down the hall inspecting the ranks of children. His glossy black hair rests perfectly styled on his head. He holds a ruler behind his back like a general holding a riding crop. His shirt sports an embroidered name: “MR. GUFF”. A child bursts from the group wailing something about freedom just as Mr. Guff passes him. Guff’s nostrils flare with the love of the chase as he sprints down the hallway. The two round a corner and various crashes and smacks echo through the building. The general populace ignores it. Finally, Tyler reaches his class. His teacher, MRS. LEONARD, excitedly introduces him.

MRS. LEONARD

Good morning class! Great news! We have a new student this morning. Why don’t you tell us your name?

TYLER

Uh..Tyler.

MRS. LEONARD

Terrific! Why don’t we assign you a buddy to help you learn the rules of your new school. Any volunteers?

The bulk of the class appears not to have heard the question. After a few quiet seconds JOEL, a boy about Tyler’s height and build, slowly begins to raise his hand. Before Mrs. Leonard can notice, JIM, a tall, skinny boy with a sleazy, greased hairdo, and a sickeningly polite demeanor stands up.

JIM

I would be honored to serve my fellow classmate in any capacity within my meager power.

MRS. LEONARD

Why thank you Jim! I’m sure you two will become fast friends.

Tyler takes his seat. She begins ranting about some meaningless subject as Jim leans in close to Tyler and whispers.

JIM

You stick close to me kid. This is a dangerous place, but I’ll show you the ropes.

INT. CAFETERIA - LUNCH

Climbing ropes hang from the ceiling of the gym/cafeteria. Tyler and Jim stand together in the slow moving lunch line.

JIM

First of all, everyone here knows me as Gentleman Jim. They call me that because I’m a gentleman. I’m a gentleman because I like to talk like a gentleman. Of course I have many other skills, all of which I find pleasure in exercising for the greater purpose of helping the less fortunate like yourself so that you might make it to the fourth grade. What do you need to know? Well, first you need to know who to hang around and who to avoid. You should hang around me, and avoid everyone else. Like I said this is a dangerous place. Step out of line and you’ll wind up in the hole. That’s the little dark room in the office with “ZERO TOLERANCE” written on it. Luckily for you it’s often occupied by a fellow by the name of JAX. They call him that because he killed a kid for beating him at a game of jacks. You watch your back when he gets out. Anyway, I’m going to be too busy to hang out with you at recess so your best bet is to join the soccer game. They’ll keep you safe, just don’t play goalie. I gotta go. Thanks a lot pal.

As Jim slips away into the crowd Tyler finally gets to the cashier. He reaches for money, and suddenly realizes why Jim thanked him.

CASHIER

Hurry up kid. We got a lot of gobs to stuff.

The line grows impatient behind him.

TYLER

I’m sorry, it’s just that that other kid. He stole my—

CASHIER

I don’t care. Either give me the money or get a move on.

Tyler cuts his losses and just goes to find a place to sit. Most tables are packed full of screaming children, but he finally finds a seat at a small table, separated from the rest. All the social rejects are exiled to this table. The whistle blows for recess.

EXT. PLAYGROUND - DAY

The storm has subsided and the sun now reveals the playgrounds full bleakness. Following Jim’s advice, Tyler makes his way to the soccer field where most players are waist deep in snow. The players stare at him, then cast inquisitive glances at each other. Finally, one of the oldest among them points to the goal.

TYLER

Oh no, I-I don’t think...

The finger stands despite his protest. Tyler succumbs, hoping for the best. He trudges to the goal box and just as he gets there he sees a stampede of far too many players for both teams. He freezes up and in a matter of seconds the ball flies right by his hand and into the net. The defensive players turn on him with furious faces. They chase him briefly, but he escapes as they return their interest to the soccer field. He looks to the tether balls. Fifth and Sixth graders dominate that court. He looks to the slide. A group of unwashed first graders guard it. He looks to the Big Toy. He sees the scavenger from earlier selling the coat he stole. Finally he spots an empty swing set. As he nears it, a cold wind make the chains creak in an eerie fashion. Tyler, undeterred, takes a seat. He nearly starts pumping when a rotund bully named THOMPSON steps in front of him.

THOMPSON

You’ve made a mistake small fry.

TYLER

What? Oh I’m sorry. Should I swing somewhere else?

THOMPSON

No, I’m afraid you don’t understand. I own these swings. All of them.

TYLER

Oh, uh, I’m sorry. I’ll leave.

THOMPSON

You just don’t get it, do you? You’re trespassing. If you trespass, then you gotta pay.

Thompson grabs Tyler’s collar when someone’s voice, stays his hand.

JOEL

Let him go.

THOMPSON

This ain’t got nothing to do with you.

Joel flicks a marble to Thompson. He inspects it, and shoves it in his pocket.

THOMPSON

It’s all yours.

Thompson wanders back to his post. Joel steps up to Tyler and jerks him out of the swing by the collar.

TYLER

Thank you. You really saved my bacon.

JOEL

No I didn’t. I just wanted to swing. If I waited until Thompson was done with you recess would be over.

TYLER

Still...I owe you one.

JOEL

You don’t owe me anything. I didn’t do you a favor. No one does anyone any favors in here.

TYLER

Well...my name’s Tyler.

Joel stops swinging.

JOEL

Look here pal. Let’s get one thing straight. I didn’t save you and I’m not your friend. I just came over here to swing, and I like to swing in silence...so buzz off.

INT. CLASSROOM - AFTERNOON

Mrs. Leonard continues raving about nothing in particular as the class buzzes. They repeat the name Jax more than any other word. Jim leans over to Tyler.

JIM

Look, I’m sorry about earlier, but I figure it was best for you to learn not to trust anyone now rather than later. No hard feelings right?

Tyler pretends to listen to Mrs. Leonard.

JIM

Alright. You’re still mad. I get that, but I got something important to tell you. Jax gets out tomorrow, see? He hates it when things change while he’s inside, so a new kid’s got a lot to worry about if you catch my drift. You listen to me Tyler. Be careful.

The bell rings and kids leap out of their desks. Tyler follows.

INT. HOUSE - EVENING

Mom and Dad sit at the dinner table enjoying an evening potable and reading material. Tyler interrupts them.

TYLER

Mom, Dad. We need to talk. I’m not going back to that school.

MOM

Oh Tyler, I’m sure you’re overreacting.

TYLER

No, I’m not. I can see where you’d think I was, but I’m really not. You saw it Dad, tell her.

Dad looks up from his document for the first time.

DAD

What’s that son? Uh, I agree with your mother.

He returns to the document.

MOM

Look, Tyler I know how tough it is to start all over at a new school—

TYLER

It’s not that, I jus—

MOM

Tyler. I’m not going to discuss this with you now. How about you go for the rest of the week, and if you still haven’t made any friends, we can talk about transferring you to another school.

Tyler sees no victory in sight, and heads off to bed.

EXT. PLAYGROUND - EARLY MORNING

Tyler rests on the outskirts of a congregation of children huddling outside the school entrance like penguins. Suddenly, another entrance bursts open with an inhuman glow emanating from within. Children scuttle away as if to avoid a predator. A silhouette breaks up the cold light. The shadow, not terribly large, but imminently imposing, drifts across the snow laden plain. The dark apparition, Jax, stops just centimeters from Tyler’s face and inhales deeply as if Tyler’s fear could nourish him. The whistle blows and Tyler washes into the sea of students. Jax remains, the children parting around him like an island in a river. His cold stare never leaves Tyler.

INT. CAFETERIA - LUNCH

Tyler, with a sack lunch, searches the tables to find Joel. Finally he sees him eating a school taco. Tyler sets a large and beautiful marble on Joel’s tray. Joel looks up at Tyler in great dismay.

JOEL

What are you doing?

TYLER

It’s my best shooter.

JOEL

I thought I told you, you don’t owe me anything.

TYLER

I know. It’s just that I won’t be needing it anymore, and I thought it should go to someone who could use it.

They stare at each other. Joel refuses to accept the marble. Tyler leaves the marble and returns to the outcast table from the day before. As he bites his PB and J, he makes eye contact with Jax across the room. Jax’s icy eyes never move. At the end of lunch the whistle blows, and Tyler walks slowly to his doom.

EXT. PLAYGROUND - DAY

Hope sneaks onto Tyler’s face as he moves toward Mr. Guff. He believes that he might find refuge if he never leaves the Vice Principal’s side. Guff’s angry eyes remain fixed on the playground before him.

MR. GUFF

I’m not going to let you loiter here all recess you know.

This devastates Tyler.

MR. GUFF

You ought to go and make some friends. Go on now. I only give orders once.

As Guff’s grip tightens around his ruler, Tyler starts toward the swings. All the children he passes shake their heads in pity. The swings still creak with ghostly sorrow. Tyler tries to get one last swing in before he dies. He grasps the cold chains and eases into the seat.

Suddenly, Jax appears from nowhere and grabs the back of his collar. He rips Tyler backwards off the swing and slams him against the frame. Jax rears back, but before he lands his blow something bounces hard off of his cheek. Jax and Tyler look down in tandem to find Tyler’s lovely shooter glistening in the sand. Jax looks in the direction the marble came from. Joel stands, sling shot ready, surrounded by a horde of kindergarteners, all with a handful of marbles. Jax turns his attention back to Tyler, and decides it’s not worth it. He begrudgingly releases Tyler and slinks back into nowhere. The kindergarteners dissipate with their bounty of marbles, and Joel allows himself a slight grin. Tyler beams with gratitude.

TYLER

I thought there were no favors in here?

Joel extends his hand.

JOEL

I’m Joel.

Tyler takes Joel’s hand with alacrity.

TYLER

So does this mean I owe you my life?

They each move to a swing.

JOEL

No, just about two hundred marbles.

They exchange a dry chuckle, and finally start to swing.
